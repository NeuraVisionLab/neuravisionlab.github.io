Geometry we can understand and change.

Reconstructing a shape is only the beginning. We want to change a component, transfer fine detail or improve a design’s performance while preserving the properties that matter.

We study implicit representations that describe geometry through a continuous function. In a signed distance field, each spatial point is assigned a signed distance to the surface, and the surface lies where that value is zero. Our work with EPFL and other collaborators asks how to make these representations controllable—by part and by geometric scale.

Keep the parts. Learn the whole.

A chair has legs, a seat and a back. A car has a body and wheels. A useful representation should expose those components while keeping their geometry compatible.

TMLR · 2025

PartSDF: a representation with components

PartSDF assigns each component its own shape code and pose, including position, rotation and scale. A shared decoder turns these into signed distance fields for the parts, which combine into the complete object.

The decoder alternates between processing parts independently and exchanging information across them. This lets a component respond to its neighbors instead of behaving like an isolated shape pasted into an assembly.

Using supervised part annotations, the method supports reconstruction, generation and controlled editing on cars, chairs and mixers. Its supervision can work with surface part labels without requiring every part to be a watertight mesh. Code and the associated dataset are publicly available.

Separate the silhouette from the details

Part boundaries are one way to organize a shape. Another is by scale: the overall form, the intermediate structure and the finer surface details.

ARXIV PREPRINT · 2026

StyleFields: coarse-to-fine shape control

A single shape code tends to entangle global form and local detail. StyleFields introduces several style codes at different depths of an implicit decoder, using adaptive normalization to modulate its features.

The training process gives these codes different responsibilities. The network grows progressively, and intermediate outputs learn smoothed versions of the target distance field. Earlier stages focus on broad structure; later stages refine detail.

This allows geometry at selected scales to be exchanged between objects: for example, retaining the coarse form of one car while borrowing finer features from another. Unlike explicit part control, this approach needs no part annotations. The public preprint evaluates reconstruction and editing on cars, chairs and airplanes.

Improve performance. Preserve design intent.

Optimization becomes more useful when we can say what may change—and what should stay fixed.

PARTSDF · 2025 & STYLEFIELDS · 2026 PREPRINT

Choosing the freedom to optimize

Both representations connect shape control to aerodynamic design. PartSDF can optimize a car’s body while fixing the shape and placement of its wheels. StyleFields can update coarse or fine style codes while holding the other scales constant.

A differentiable surrogate predicts aerodynamic performance and supplies gradients for changing the representation. The choice of editable variables matters: unconstrained changes to a global code may alter the entire vehicle, while part- or scale-specific updates preserve more of the intended design.

These are studies of learned design optimization. A lower surrogate-predicted drag value is not, by itself, a physical validation. Our complementary work on uncertainty in iterative networks explores how uncertainty can help choose designs for further simulation.
